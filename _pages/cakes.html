I have always loved decorating cakes, so each time I publish a new measurement, I have gotten into the habit of making an edible diagram of the collision system in cake form! Here are pictures of those cakes, along with a few that are some of my favorites from over the years (with varying amounts of physics content).

Photonuclear Jets

A diagram of photonuclear jet production in cake form. Each cake represents a colliding lead ion, where chocolate frosting corresponds to neutrons and vanilla frosting to protons (due to the greater number of neutrons, the correspondence was chosen based on the baker's preferred frosting flavor). The yellow frosting demonstrates the coherent emission of a photon by one nucleus, which strikes the other, producing two collimated sprays of hadrons (cherries), which correspond to jets. The strawberries indicate additional particle production from the fragmentation of the struck nucleus.

Non-UPC Dimuons

A diagram of di-muon photoproduction in a peripheral Pb+Pb collision in cake form. Each cake represents a colliding lead ion, where chocolate frosting corresponds to neutrons and vanilla frosting to protons (due to the greater number of neutrons, the correspondence was chosen based on the baker's preferred frosting flavor). Red-orange frosting in the overlap region represents the hot, dense quark-gluon plasma formed in heavy ion collisions. The blue lines indicate a pair of muons produced via two-photon fusion. Since the muons traverse different path lengths in the plasma, it is possible to use their kinematic correlations as a probe of the electromagnetic properties of the plasma.

Return from Lockdown

A cake celebrating the official return to the office at Columbia after the COVID-19 lockdowns. The number of neutrons (read) and protons (blue) in the colliding ions do not correspond to the exact proportions or spatial distributions for lead (I'm not that crazy).
